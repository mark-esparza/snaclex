The workbench itself ships with no example structures, no suggested chemicals and no pre-filled fields: what to analyze is entirely your choice. Everything that would otherwise have been a hint or a demo button lives on this page instead.

On this page: Workflow · What each analysis does · Reading the numbers · Validating the method · Interaction panels · Glossary · Data sources · Limitations

Workflow

  1. Load a structure. Type a 4-character PDB ID to load it directly, or type a protein name to full-text search the PDB and pick from the results. You can also upload your own PDB or mmCIF file; it is held in memory for your session and never written to disk.
  2. Pick a bound molecule (step 2 in the left column) to profile every atomic contact it makes with the protein. If the structure is apo — no bound ligand — skip this and use Pockets.
  3. Run the analyses you need. Each tab is independent; nothing is computed until you ask for it.
  4. Compile a report on the Report tab, which aggregates everything run this session and exports to TXT, JSON, CSV or PDB with full method provenance.

Large assemblies are offered per chain rather than refused, so a structure over the atom limit will ask you which chain to load.

Reading the numbers

Docking score. An AutoDock-style grid score in relative units where lower is better. It is not calibrated to kcal/mol and is not an affinity. It is comparable between ligands docked into the same site in the same run, and not comparable across different sites.

Ligand efficiency. Score divided by heavy-atom count. Use this rather than the raw score when comparing molecules of different sizes, since a bigger molecule accumulates a better raw score simply by having more atoms.

Pocket druggability. A geometric heuristic from volume, enclosure and lining-residue composition. It says a cavity is shaped like pockets that tend to bind drugs; it does not say anything binds there.

Conservation. Column-wise conservation in the Pfam family alignment. A conserved residue is under selective pressure, which often but not always means functional importance.

Panel z-scores. See Interaction panels — these are panel-relative contrasts, not affinities.

Every analysis carries a Methods & reproducibility block with the tool version, parameters, random seed and limitations behind that specific result. Open it before citing a number.

Validating the method

The Benchmark tab answers "does this tool actually recover an answer we already know?". Load a structure that has a bound ligand and benchmark it: SnaCleX re-docks that ligand into its own crystallographic site and reports whether the pocket was found, the pose RMSD against the deposited coordinates, how many of the real interactions were recovered, and whether the pose is physically plausible.

Interaction panels

A panel is a matrix question: dock every ligand of a curated system into every target in it, in one run. A curated system is a versioned description of one piece of biology — its proteins, their sites, and the molecules that act on them.

Cells show z-scores, not raw scores, and this matters. Raw scores are not comparable across targets: a deeper pocket scores better for any ligand, so reading a raw matrix across a row would rank pocket burial rather than preference. Ligand efficiency is therefore standardized within each target column (higher = better than that target's panel average), and only those standardized values are compared along a row to give the selectivity gap. Every z-score is relative to the panel you submitted and changes if the panel changes.

Read the verification banner. A curated system asserts things you cannot check by running the code: that a PDB entry really is the protein named, that its site ligand is really deposited, that the UniProt mapping holds. A system marked DRAFT has not had those assertions checked against RCSB. Columns are also marked where a declared cofactor is missing from the entry, which means that site is being scored as an empty cavity.

Glossary

apo / holoA structure solved without a bound ligand (apo) or with one (holo).
B-factorCrystallographic temperature factor: how much an atom's position varies. High B means flexible or poorly ordered.
CofactorA non-protein molecule an enzyme needs to function (FAD, SAM, PLP, a metal ion). SnaCleX excludes these from docking grids unless a curated system declares them.
ConformerOne 3D arrangement of a flexible molecule. SnaCleX docks a single PubChem conformer rigidly.
EnclosureHow buried a cavity is — the fraction of directions from its centre that hit protein.
Heavy atomAny atom that is not hydrogen. Crystal structures rarely resolve hydrogens, so all geometry here is heavy-atom.
Ligand efficiencyScore per heavy atom; controls for molecular size.
LIGSITEThe grid-scanning algorithm used for cavity detection.
mmCIFThe PDB's modern structure format, used for entries too large for the legacy PDB format.
PfamA database of protein family alignments, used here for conservation scoring.
RMSDRoot-mean-square deviation between two sets of coordinates. Under 2 Å is the usual threshold for "the pose was recovered".
Salt bridgeAn electrostatic contact between oppositely charged groups.
z-scoreHow far a value sits from the mean, in standard deviations. In panels, computed within one target's column.

Data sources

SnaCleX has no database of its own. Every result is assembled live from public services, so the tool is only as available as they are. You can check all of them at once at /api/selftest.

Limitations

SnaCleX is an interpretation workbench, not a drug-discovery pipeline, and every number it produces is a heuristic computed from a single static structure. In particular:

  • Docking treats both the receptor and the ligand as rigid. Induced fit, side-chain rearrangement and ligand strain are ignored.
  • There are no explicit hydrogens and no protonation or tautomer enumeration, so the electrostatics of charged groups are approximated.
  • Scores are not affinities and are not calibrated to kcal/mol.
  • Waters, ions and cofactors are excluded from scoring grids unless a curated system declares them, so a cofactor-dependent site may be scored in an unphysical empty state.
  • Conservation depends on the family alignment available for that protein; a small or skewed family gives a weak signal.
  • Nothing here models concentration, time, competition or expression.

Research use only. None of this is validated affinity data and none of it is clinical guidance. Confirm anything you intend to act on with orthogonal evidence and experiment.
